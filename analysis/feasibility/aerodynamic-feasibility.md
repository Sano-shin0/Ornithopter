# ORNITHOPTER Aerodynamic Feasibility

**Status:** DRAFT
**Revision:** 0.1

---

# 1. Objective

Determine whether the proposed flapping-wing configuration can generate sufficient aerodynamic forces for controlled flight.

---

# 2. Fundamental Requirement

For sustained flight, the average vertical aerodynamic force must approximately balance the system weight:

$$
\overline{L}\approx mg
$$

For accelerating or climbing flight:

$$
\overline{L}>mg
$$

may be required depending on the flight condition.

---

# 3. Flapping-Wing Nature

ORNITHOPTER shall be treated as an unsteady aerodynamic system.

The aerodynamic forces are expected to vary during each wingbeat.

Therefore:

$$
L(t)\neq constant
$$

and:

$$
T(t)\neq constant
$$

where:

* \(L(t)\) is instantaneous aerodynamic lift,
* \(T(t)\) is instantaneous propulsive force.

The quantities of primary interest for sustained flight are their cycle averages:

$$
\overline{L}
=
\frac{1}{T}
\int_0^T L(t)\,dt
$$

$$
\overline{T}
=
\frac{1}{T}
\int_0^T T(t)\,dt
$$

# 5. Preliminary Force Model

A first-order estimate may use:

$$
L
=
\frac12\rho V^2SC_L
$$

and:

$$
D
=
\frac12\rho V^2SC_D
$$

However, these equations shall not be considered sufficient as the final aerodynamic model because ORNITHOPTER operates with strongly unsteady wing motion.

They are useful for establishing initial order-of-magnitude estimates.

---

# 6. Flapping Velocity

A characteristic wing velocity shall be estimated from:

$$
U_{flap}\sim 2\Phi fR
$$

where:

* \(\Phi\) is the angular stroke amplitude in radians,
* \(f\) is the flapping frequency,
* \(R\) is a characteristic distance from the wing root.

The exact kinematic definition shall be established when the wing motion is modeled.

---

# 7. Relative Velocity

The effective velocity experienced by a wing element may depend on both:

* translational flight velocity,
* flapping velocity.

A simplified characteristic velocity may therefore be expressed as:

$$
U_{eff}
\sim
\sqrt{V^2+U_{flap}^2}
$$

This is an initial approximation only.

---

# 8. Reynolds Number

The Reynolds number shall be evaluated because the aerodynamic regime is expected to differ significantly from conventional large aircraft.

$$
Re=
\frac{\rho UL}{\mu}
$$

where:

* \(\rho\) = air density,
* \(U\) = characteristic velocity,
* \(L\) = characteristic length,
* \(\mu\) = dynamic viscosity.

The characteristic velocity and length shall be defined consistently for each aerodynamic model.

---

# 9. Key Aerodynamic Questions

The analysis shall determine:

1. Required average lift.
2. Required average thrust.
3. Required wing area.
4. Required flapping frequency.
5. Required stroke amplitude.
6. Expected aerodynamic efficiency.
7. Effect of wing flexibility.
8. Effect of wing pitch.
9. Effect of forward velocity.
10. Transition between flapping and gliding.

---

# 10. Gliding Analysis

The gliding configuration shall be analyzed separately.

During gliding, the system shall rely primarily on aerodynamic forces generated by forward motion rather than continuous active flapping.

Important quantities include:

$$
\frac{L}{D}
$$

glide angle,

sink rate,

and required airspeed.

The transition between flapping and gliding shall be investigated as an energy-management problem.

---

# 12. Validation

Aerodynamic predictions shall progressively be validated through:

1. analytical calculations,
2. numerical models,
3. static experiments,
4. force measurements,
5. mechanism testing,
6. and free-flight experiments.

Aerodynamic feasibility is currently unresolved.
