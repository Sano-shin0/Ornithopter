# Research Rules

## 1. Purpose

These rules govern how technical information is searched, evaluated, recorded, and used in the ORNITHOPTER project.

---

## 2. Research Question First

Research must begin with a clearly defined question.

Bad:

> Research bird flight.

Good:

> Determine the expected Reynolds-number range for the wing during the target flight condition.

---

## 3. Source Hierarchy

When possible, prefer sources in the following order:

1. Peer-reviewed scientific literature
2. University or research institution publications
3. Government and aerospace organizations
4. International standards and recognized technical organizations
5. Manufacturer technical documentation
6. Engineering textbooks
7. Technical reports
8. Reputable engineering websites
9. Forums and community discussions

Lower-quality sources may provide useful leads but should not automatically be treated as authoritative evidence.

---

## 4. Source Traceability

Every important external claim must be traceable to its source.

Record:

* title,
* author or organization,
* publication date when available,
* URL or identifier,
* relevant section/page,
* and the specific information used.

---

## 5. Applicability

Finding a source does not prove that its result applies to ORNITHOPTER.

For each important source, evaluate:

* geometry,
* scale,
* Reynolds number,
* Mach number,
* operating conditions,
* material,
* mechanism,
* flight regime,
* assumptions,
* and experimental setup.

---

## 6. Conflicting Sources

If sources disagree:

1. record the disagreement,
2. compare their assumptions,
3. compare their experimental or analytical methods,
4. determine their applicability,
5. identify the most relevant evidence,
6. preserve the uncertainty if it cannot be resolved.

---

## 7. Research Output

Research documents should clearly separate:

### Established Facts

Information directly supported by reliable evidence.

### Interpretation

Reasoned conclusions derived from evidence.

### Assumptions

Values or conditions introduced because information is unavailable.

### Open Questions

Questions that remain unresolved.

---

## 8. No Source Fabrication

Never invent:

* paper titles,
* authors,
* DOI numbers,
* URLs,
* publication dates,
* experimental results,
* or quotations.

If a source cannot be verified, mark it as unverified.

---

## 9. Research-to-Engineering Link

Research should not end with a collection of references.

The output must explain:

> What does this information change about the engineering problem?

Whenever possible, conclude with:

* design implication,
* calculation implication,
* simulation implication,
* testing implication,
* or remaining research question.

---

## 10. Research Quality

Prefer a small number of highly relevant sources over a large collection of weak or unrelated sources.

Research should reduce uncertainty rather than merely increase documentation volume.
